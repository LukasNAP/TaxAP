# VA — findings

Status: **Layer 2 investigated (2026-08-26), not ready to build.** A+ has 106 real `VA###` tax-body codes covering 822 active VA ship-tos, and the coding convention is clean and human-readable (`TBTXNAM` = `"Virginia <Locality>"`) — closer to NC/NV-style than GA-style for the large majority of localities. But Virginia has a structural wrinkle GA/NC/NV don't: **four of its counties share an exact name with a legally separate, independently-taxed city** (Fairfax, Franklin, Richmond, Roanoke), and live A+ data shows at least one of these pairs (Richmond) with a real rate split (5.3% vs 6.0%) concentrated overwhelmingly on the code that lacks the disambiguating "(city)" suffix — a strong, numerically-backed lead that ship-to-to-locality assignment needs a human/address check before any comparison is trusted, not just a code-name-to-jurisdiction join. No official-source adapter exists yet (`machine-readable-source` status per `docs/roadmap-50-states.md`), so rate comparability against `tax.virginia.gov`'s XLSX is **not yet confirmable** through the app — but independent web verification against Virginia Tax's own bulletins found A+'s current rates internally consistent with every real regional/local-option overlay checked, except the Richmond anomaly above.

## Address matching

**Mostly NC-style (direct code-to-locality mapping, human-readable `TBTXNAM`), but with a real, confirmed exception that makes "address matching not needed" the wrong blanket answer for VA.**

- 106 distinct `VA###` codes are assigned to active ship-tos, each named plainly `"Virginia <County/City>"`. Virginia has 95 counties + 38 independent cities = **133 real county-equivalents** (Census Gazetteer convention); A+'s 106 codes are a plausible, non-sparse subset of that (roughly 80%), consistent with "codes exist where Atlantic has active customers," not a coverage gap the way AL/CO/MO's sparse ~50-90-code sets were.
- **The real complication: Virginia is the only state where a county and an independent city can legally share the same name and be taxed differently.** Live data shows exactly this for two of the four known name-duplicate pairs:
  - **Fairfax** — `VA029` "Virginia Fairfax" (42 ship-tos, county) and `VA234` "Virginia Fairfax (city)" (2 ship-tos) both currently total 6.0% — no live discrepancy today, but only because both happen to sit in the Northern Virginia regional-tax footprint.
  - **Franklin** — `VA033` "Virginia Franklin" (4 ship-tos, county, 5.3%) vs. `VA235` "Virginia Franklin (city)" (1 ship-to, 6.0%) — correctly different, since Franklin County is outside the Hampton Roads region and the City of Franklin is inside it. This pair is internally consistent with real VA law.
  - **Roanoke** — `VA077` "Virginia Roanoke" (26 ship-tos, county, 5.3%) vs. `VA217` "Virginia Roanoke (city)" (5 ship-tos, 5.3%) — same rate today, no live discrepancy.
  - **Richmond — the one that looks wrong.** `VA076` "Virginia Richmond" (**59 ship-tos — the single largest tax-body bucket in the entire state**, 5.3%) vs. `VA216` "Virginia Richmond (city)" (7 ship-tos, 6.0%). Richmond County (rural Northern Neck, population ~7,000, not in any regional-tax footprint) is genuinely a 5.3% jurisdiction; the independent City of Richmond (the state capital, in the Central Virginia Region) is genuinely 6.0% — both rates are individually correct for what each code names. **But it is not plausible that Atlantic's single largest VA ship-to concentration is physically located in rural Richmond County** rather than the state capital. This reads as ship-tos that are actually in the City of Richmond being coded to the county-named body (no "(city)" suffix) and taxed at the county's lower rate. Not confirmed — no address-level data was pulled for this investigation — but the volume skew (59 vs. 7, inverted from what Richmond County's real population would predict) is a strong, specific lead. **Flag as ambiguous, needs a human/address-level check — do not assume it's correct, and do not assume it's wrong.**
  - A fifth historical pair, **Bedford**, no longer applies the same way: the independent City of Bedford reverted to a town within Bedford County in 2013. `VA240` "Virginia Bedford (city)" (1 ship-to) still exists in A+ and coincidentally matches Bedford County's rate (5.3% = 5.3%) today, so there's no live rate discrepancy, but the code is a legacy label for a place that hasn't been a separate taxing jurisdiction in over a decade.
- **Beyond the name-duplicate pairs, a broader structural risk (not confirmed with live ZIP-level data in this pass):** Virginia's four regional sales-tax overlays (Northern Virginia, Hampton Roads, Central Virginia, Historic Triangle) are defined by county/city boundary, not by ZIP code, and Virginia ZIP codes commonly straddle county lines the way they do in every other state investigated. A ZIP-only assignment (rather than a real street-address lookup) could plausibly misassign a ship-to sitting near a regional boundary. This wasn't tested directly here — flagged as a real risk to check before trusting a ZIP-based build, not a confirmed live finding the way Richmond is.
- No `E`-suffixed equipment-tax variant codes found. No third-tier "police jurisdiction"-style split found (VA's 6.3% cluster below is a different mechanism, not a third tax tier — see Rate comparability).

## Rate comparability

**Not yet confirmable through the app — no official-source adapter is wired for VA** (`server/fetch-official-rates.mjs VA` correctly exits with "no official-source adapter is wired for VA yet"; VA sits at `machine-readable-source` status in `docs/roadmap-50-states.md`, source found but not built). The task's freshness concern (tax.virginia.gov's own XLSX metadata shows last-modified 2023-07-14, three years old with no visible "last updated" flag) could not be resolved by re-fetching that specific file directly (`WebFetch` on the lookup page returned only the page shell, not the workbook's live metadata) — **still an open item, not resolved**, though independent web verification below gives reasonable confidence the underlying rate structure hasn't materially changed since 2023.

**Independent cross-check against Virginia Tax's own published bulletins (not the stale XLSX) found A+'s live rates internally consistent with every real regional/local-option overlay checked:**
- Base statewide (4.3% state + 1% mandatory local = 5.3%): confirmed correct for the ~85 A+ codes showing this total.
- Northern Virginia Region (+0.7% transportation, total 6.0% — Arlington, Alexandria, Fairfax Co./City, Falls Church, Loudoun, Manassas, Manassas Park, Prince William): every NoVA-named A+ code checked (`VA007` Arlington, `VA029`/`VA234` Fairfax, `VA053` Loudoun, `VA074` Prince William, `VA200` Alexandria, `VA226` Falls Church, `VA242` Manassas) matches 6.0% exactly.
- Central Virginia Region (+0.7%, total 6.0% — Richmond city, Ashland, Charles City, Chesterfield, Goochland, Hanover, Henrico, New Kent, Powhatan): every Central-VA-named A+ code with active ship-tos (`VA019` Charles City, `VA021` Chesterfield, `VA037` Goochland, `VA042` Hanover, `VA043` Henrico, `VA072` Powhatan, `VA216` Richmond (city)) matches 6.0% exactly. (New Kent has no active A+ ship-tos today, so nothing to check there.)
- Hampton Roads Region (+0.7%, total 6.0% — Chesapeake, Franklin city, Hampton, Isle of Wight, Newport News, Norfolk, Poquoson, Portsmouth, Southampton, Suffolk, Virginia Beach): every code checked (`VA046`, `VA084`, `VA207`, `VA211`, `VA212`, `VA214`, `VA220`, `VA228`, `VA235`, `VA236`, `VA244`) matches 6.0% exactly.
- Historic Triangle (regional 1% on top of the Hampton-Roads-region base, total 7.0% — James City Co., Williamsburg, York Co.): `VA047`, `VA095`, `VA221` all match 7.0% exactly.
- **A distinct, separately-legislated local-option 1% tax** (not one of the four regions above) applies in Danville (city), and Charlotte, Halifax, Henry, Northampton, and Patrick counties, per Virginia Tax Bulletins 21-6 and 22-7 (effective dates 2020–2022). A+'s codes for all six of these (`VA205` Danville, `VA020` Charlotte, `VA041` Halifax, `VA044` Henry, `VA061` Northampton, `VA068` Patrick) correctly show 6.3% — this is real and A+ has it right, not a comparability gap. (A seventh locality with this same tax, Gloucester County, has no active A+ ship-tos today, so nothing to check there.)
- **The one confirmed exception to "everything checked matches": the Richmond pair described above** — not a rate-table error (both 5.3% and 6.0% are correct numbers for their respective real jurisdictions), but a likely locality-assignment problem sitting on real, large-volume, live traffic.

**Forward-looking freshness flag, not yet a live discrepancy:** Virginia's 2026 General Assembly budget bill newly authorized *every* county and city in the state to hold a local referendum for an *additional* 1% sales tax dedicated to school construction/renovation (this expands a previously narrower authorization). As of this investigation (2026-08-26), this is an active, in-progress political process — a Cardinal News report dated 2026-08-10 describes multiple Virginia localities (including Powhatan and Wythe County, both of which have active A+ codes: `VA072` and `VA094`) racing to place this on the **November 2026** ballot. No referendum has passed yet as of today, so no A+ rate is stale because of this today — but if any of these pass, the affected localities' real rates will increase by up to 1% on some future effective date, and this is a new, still-forming source of local-rate fragmentation beyond the four regions and six-county local-option cluster already confirmed above. Worth tracking before/when a VA adapter is built, not before.

## Do-not-use and cross-context findings

**No `VA000`-shaped DO-NOT-USE placeholder appeared in the returned list** (VA's codes run `VA001`–`VA244` with no `VA000` bucket, unlike AL000/MN000/ND000/NE000/UT000/WA000/NV000). **This could not be independently confirmed as "doesn't exist" rather than "exists but was silently excluded"** — `readStateDetail`'s retired-tax-body filter runs before this investigation ever sees the data, and this project's read-only rule blocks running the underlying raw `XATXBD` query directly to check. Flagging as an open question per the project's exclusion-transparency rule, not asserting a number that isn't visible from here.

**One cross-context ship-to found:** `NC092` ("North Carolina Wake", 1 active ship-to) appears in the VA-state-filtered pull — a VA-located active ship-to (`SASHST = 'VA'`) carrying a North Carolina tax body, the same `SASTXB`-crosses-state-lines pattern already documented for GA (NC060/NC041/SC126/CA1163/PA000) and NV (DR000/NCPRST). This is almost certainly a border-customer situation (Wake Co., NC is not adjacent to VA, so this may be a genuine misassignment rather than a border case — worth a closer look, but out of scope for this pass). **Exclude this 1 ship-to from any VA-specific rate comparison**, the same way GA's and NV's cross-context outliers are excluded.

No wrong-state contamination was found *within* the `VA###`-coded rows themselves (i.e., no VA code whose `TBTXNAM` names a different state, the SC3622-shaped trap) — every `VA###` description reads "Virginia <Locality>."

## Open questions / do instead if building

- **Before building:** get a human/address-level check on the Richmond pair (`VA076` vs `VA216`) — the volume skew (59 vs. 7) toward the lower-rate, non-"(city)"-suffixed code is a real, specific, numerically-backed lead that a large share of Atlantic's VA business may be taxed at 5.3% when it should be 6.0%, but this investigation did not (and per project scope, should not) look at individual ship-to addresses to confirm it.
- **Before building:** resolve whether a `VA000`-shaped DO-NOT-USE placeholder exists in the underlying `XATXBD` table with real active ship-tos silently excluded from the 822 total shown here — this investigation could not see past `readStateDetail`'s built-in exclusion using only the vetted read-only script.
- **Before building:** decide how to handle the 1 cross-context ship-to (`NC092`) — exclude from VA findings with a visible count, same treatment as GA's and NV's cross-state outliers.
- **No adapter exists yet** — Step 3/4 (build) work is blocked on writing a VA official-source adapter against `tax.virginia.gov`'s XLSX. Before trusting that file, re-verify its actual last-modified date directly (this pass could not get past the page shell via automated fetch) — independent web verification of Virginia Tax's own bulletins suggests the underlying rate structure hasn't materially changed since 2023, but the file itself was not re-fetched and inspected directly.
- **When building the adapter, budget for VA's real jurisdiction-type variety being wider than a flat county+city join:** four regional overlays (Northern VA, Hampton Roads, Central VA, Historic Triangle) plus a separate six-locality (soon possibly seven, with Gloucester) local-option +1% cluster plus VA's county/city name-duplication (Fairfax, Franklin, Richmond, Roanoke, historically Bedford) — a naive "match code to Census place name" join needs to disambiguate county vs. city explicitly, not just match on name.
- **Track the 2026 school-construction local-option referendum wave** (enabled statewide by the 2026–28 budget bill; multiple localities including Powhatan and Wythe — both with active A+ codes — pursuing a November 2026 ballot measure as of a 2026-08-10 report). Nothing is stale today because of this, but it is a real, currently-forming source of near-term rate fragmentation beyond what's captured above.
- Use `SASTXB` (ship-to level), not `CMTXBD` (customer level) — not independently re-verified for VA in this pass, but consistent with the pattern already established for every other state investigated.
